Final Project: The final project is a major component of the course and is intended to provide hands-on experience conducting research in computer vision or a closely related area.

  • Teams of approximately 3-5 students
  • Three main milestones:
    • Project proposal / literature review
    • Mid-project presentation + report
    • Final poster presentation + final report
  • Project ideas and team formation will be supported through Piazza.
  • Once your team is formed, please register it using the project sign-up sheet (link TBA) and list all team members.
  • Tentative timeline:
    • Proposal: late September
    • Mid-project: early November
    • Poster session: Dec 7
    • Report submission: Dec 9
  • Due to the large number of students, we plan to have one dedicated project presentation day, while the remaining presentations will be scheduled during extra time in regular lectures.
  • Exact deadlines and presentation schedules will be announced separately.

Project Proposal & Mid-Project Presentation:

  • 7 min: presentation
  • 3 min: Q&A and feedback
  • Proposal: problem/motivation, related work, and the overall direction or ideas you are considering.
  • Mid-project: method, progress so far, experimental setup, planned baselines and evaluation metrics, preliminary results if available, and remaining work / next steps.

Mid-Project Report: The mid-project report should summarize the project direction and planned experiments. It should include:

  • Introduction
  • Related Work
  • Planned Method
  • Experimental Plan
    • datasets
    • baselines
    • evaluation metrics
    • planned comparisons / ablations
    • expected tables or figures

Results are not required at this stage, but the experimental setup should be concrete enough that the main experiments are clearly defined.

Final Report: The final report should follow a CVPR-style paper format and include, at minimum:

  • Abstract
  • Introduction
  • Related Work
  • Method
  • Experiments
  • Conclusion and Limitations

The report should clearly describe the problem, technical approach, experimental setup, results, analysis, and limitations of the project. At the end of the report, include a brief contribution statement indicating the specific component(s) of the project led by each team member. Every team member should be responsible for at least one specific and meaningful part of the project.
